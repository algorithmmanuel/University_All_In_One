[fundamentals]

Why use multiple complementary controls?|One control can fail or cover only some paths|Multiple controls guarantee safety|Monitoring replaces access controls|Layered controls address different failure modes; their effectiveness still needs verification.
What does residual risk mean?|Risk remaining after controls are applied|Risk that must always be zero|The number of installed tools|Controls can reduce risk without removing it; remaining exposure needs accountable decisions.
Which risk response transfers some financial consequences?|An appropriate insurance arrangement|Deleting incident records|Disabling monitoring|Insurance may transfer defined financial consequences but does not remove operational responsibility or all loss.
A game reports a high score. What does this establish?|Performance under that simulation’s rules|Professional competence in every environment|That every security control is effective|A simulated result is bounded evidence; it does not certify broad mastery or operational assurance.

[identity]

What should a user do with an MFA approval they did not initiate?|Deny it and review account activity through official controls|Approve it to stop the notification|Send the code to the caller|An unexpected approval can authorise someone else’s access; repetition does not make it legitimate.

What is a recovery code?|A secret used to regain account access|A public troubleshooting identifier|A message safe to post in group chat|Recovery codes can enable account access and must be protected like other authentication secrets.

Why prefer individual accounts over a shared administrator login?|Actions can be attributed and access revoked individually|Individual accounts eliminate all compromise|Shared accounts always have stronger authentication|Individual identities support accountability and lifecycle controls; they still need protection.

What should happen before deploying a restrictive network change?|Check dependencies, authorisation and a rollback plan|Apply it everywhere without testing|Delete all previous configurations|Reviewed, scoped changes reduce accidental outages and support recovery if the rule is wrong.

Why test and plan important patches?|Changes may affect compatibility and availability|Testing makes patches unnecessary|Patches never affect running services|Risk-based testing and scheduling balance security improvement with operational continuity.
