[fundamentals]
A spreadsheet contains customer names and account numbers. What is this in a risk assessment?|An information asset requiring protection|A threat actor|A security treatment|Information is an asset with value and protection needs. A threat actor can harm it; a treatment changes the risk.
A team identifies a deliberate attack and an accidental deletion. What do both demonstrate?|Threat events can be intentional or accidental|Only deliberate actions can create security risk|Accidental deletion cannot affect availability|Risk assessment should include relevant mistakes and accidents as well as deliberate action. Neither unlimited collection nor indiscriminate deletion is a sound handling rule.

Which asset inventory entry most helps route a security decision?|Service owner and business purpose|Desktop background colour|Purchase invoice font|Ownership and purpose connect technical assets to accountable decisions. Cosmetic details do not identify that responsibility.
Which is an example of preventive physical security?|A locked equipment cabinet with controlled keys|An incident report written afterwards|A restoration exercise|The lock restricts physical access before an event. Reporting records an event and restoration exercises test recovery.
Which activity is primarily detective?|Reviewing unexpected privileged access alerts|Removing an unused network path|Restoring a damaged database|Alert review looks for an event. Removing paths is preventive and restoring a database is corrective.
Why classify information before deciding how to share it?|Protection should reflect sensitivity and intended recipients|Classification automatically encrypts the file|Every classification requires public publication|Classification guides handling decisions. It neither performs encryption nor authorises publication by itself.
An employee leaves a customer list on a shared printer. What is the immediate concern?|Unauthorised people may view the information|The printer necessarily altered the data|The customer service must be offline|The stated exposure concerns disclosure. What does a business impact analysis help identify?|Consequences and priorities when services are disrupted|The exact identity of every future attacker|The correct patch for every software flaw|Business impact analysis informs continuity priorities. Why include contractors in security responsibilities?|Their access and activities can affect organisational assets|Employment status automatically grants trust|Only permanent staff can cause an incident|Responsibilities follow access and risk. Which is a risk treatment rather than a risk description?|Remove an unnecessary public administration interface|A stolen password could expose records|A flood may interrupt a branch|Removing the interface changes exposure. The other statements describe possible events and consequences.
What does security by design require during a new feature build?|Consider threats, authority and failure behaviour while designing and implementing|Wait until deployment to consider access rules|Treat a final scan as the entire design process|Design choices shape exposure and failure modes. What is the main purpose of security awareness reporting routes?|Help people obtain guidance and report concerns promptly|Replace every technical access control|Turn every suspicious event into confirmed fraud|Reporting enables coordinated action. It supplements technical controls and does not itself establish wrongdoing.

A search advertisement offers urgent password recovery. What should you do if a suspicious page begins downloading a file?|Avoid running it and follow the approved reporting process|Open it to learn whether it is harmful|Rename it and assume the risk is gone|Do not execute untrusted downloads for curiosity. A new filename does not establish safe contents.
A payment approval request arrives through a personal chat account. What should govern the decision?|The established business approval process|The account profile photograph alone|Whether the sender uses friendly language|Profile details and tone can be imitated. Consequential approvals need the organisation's established verification and authority checks.
Why can a familiar shared-document service still deliver a malicious request?|An attacker can misuse a legitimate hosting service|All documents on familiar services are unsafe|Hosting providers approve every business instruction|Legitimate infrastructure may host deceptive content. The request still needs validation without declaring every hosted document malicious.
A recruiter requests identity documents before explaining the employer. What is prudent?|Verify the organisation and justified need before sharing sensitive data|Send everything because recruitment requires unlimited data|Use a public social post to supply the documents|Verification and minimisation reduce disclosure risk. Recruitment does not justify unlimited collection or public sharing.
A user typed a password into a fake page but says sign-in failed. What should response assume?|The submitted password may still have been captured|Failure proves the password never left the device|Only successful sign-ins can expose passwords|A deceptive page can capture input before displaying failure. Exposure assessment should not depend only on the displayed outcome.
An attacker impersonates support inside an ongoing real ticket. What should users verify?|The support identity and requested action through established controls|Only the ticket subject line|Whether the attacker knows the last reply|Access to conversational context increases plausibility but does not establish authority to collect secrets or bypass controls.
A campaign uses images instead of ordinary text. What should the defender consider?|Detection and user guidance must account for image-based requests|Image content cannot contain deceptive instructions|Every image must be deleted from every message|Images can carry persuasive instructions. Proportionate detection and verification are more useful than assuming safety or banning all images.
A phishing report includes credentials in the incident ticket. What should responders do?|Restrict exposure, handle the secret through approved controls and avoid further copying|Forward the entire ticket to a public forum|Assume a private ticket makes rotation unnecessary|Incident records need data protection too. Restricting distribution and addressing the exposed secret matter even when reporting was well intended.
A simulation has a low click rate but staff use the message's phone number for verification. What was missed?|Whether verification used an independent trustworthy channel|Measure only the speed of reported link blocking|Accept any phone call as independent verification|Calling an attacker-supplied number preserves attacker control. A mail gateway confirms a sender domain passed authentication. What does that leave unresolved?|Whether the authenticated sender's request is legitimate and authorised|That domain authentication is equivalent to payment approval|That authenticated messages cannot originate from compromised accounts|Domain authentication addresses origin checks, not the business legitimacy of every request from that domain, including compromised accounts.
A fake helpdesk asks users to approve an application consent screen. [identity]
What is a passphrase?|A password formed from a sequence of words or similar memorable text|A public account identifier|A list of approved network ports|A passphrase is an authentication secret. Usernames identify accounts and port lists describe connectivity.
Why use a password manager with appropriate protections?|To help maintain distinct strong credentials without memorising every one|To make sharing the vault password necessary|To remove the need to protect recovery access|A protected manager supports unique secrets. Its vault and recovery process still need strong protection.
What does identity federation let a relying service do?|Use assertions from a trusted identity provider under defined rules|Trust any identity claim from any website|Avoid validating the intended audience of an assertion|Federation depends on an explicit trust and validation relationship. It does not permit arbitrary issuers or remove audience checks.
What should happen to a default installation password?|Change it through the approved initial configuration process|Keep it because the vendor chose it|Publish it so future maintainers can find it|Defaults may be widely known. Replace and protect them; documentation should describe access procedures rather than disclose secrets.
What is a role used for in access management?|Grouping permissions appropriate to a defined function|Guaranteeing every member behaves honestly|Encrypting the database automatically|Roles organise authority. They do not prove intent or perform encryption on their own.
What is an account lockout or throttling control intended to limit?|Repeated authentication attempts under defined rules|Every misuse by an already authenticated user|The sensitivity of stored business data|Authentication attempt controls can reduce guessing but need availability considerations. They do not govern all authorised-session actions or classify data.

What is appropriate?|Request approved access for the business need|Borrow someone else's session|Change the record owner in a browser request|Use accountable provisioning. What does a joiner process need before granting access?|A verified identity and approved role requirements|Only a new email display name|A copy of another worker's full permissions|Provisioning should follow verified identity and justified duties. What is a credential-stuffing risk?|Reused leaked credentials being tried against other services|A user storing an approved passphrase in a protected manager|Every legitimate automated service login|Credential reuse enables cross-service attempts. Protected storage and approved automation are different activities.
What is an access recertification?|A periodic review of whether existing permissions remain justified|A guarantee that all passwords were changed|A replacement for every login check|Review examines continued need and ownership. It does not inherently rotate secrets or enforce sign-in.

Why should a reset link have a limited lifetime and single-use behaviour?|To constrain how long and how often it can grant recovery access|To make it safe to publish the link|To replace verification of the target account|A reset token is a temporary secret granting authority. Expiry and one-time use reduce exposure but do not justify disclosure or remove account binding.
Why is an account's display name insufficient to identify its owner?|Names can be duplicated or changed|Display names cannot contain letters|Every account always belongs to one device|Use stable identity records and appropriate verification. A human-readable label alone can be ambiguous.
What is a service identity?|An identity used by a program or automated service|Necessarily a shared human administrator login|A physical visitor badge only|Programs need identities and scoped permissions. Their lifecycle differs from ordinary human or physical access.
What does re-authentication before a sensitive account change help establish?|Fresh assurance that the current user may proceed with that action|Proof that every earlier session was legitimate|Automatic approval for another account's changes|Fresh verification can protect a sensitive transition. It does not rewrite session history or establish authority over other users.
Why avoid placing access tokens in screenshots sent for support?|Anyone with a usable token may gain its associated access|A screenshot always revokes the token|Tokens only matter when printed on paper|Tokens can carry authority while valid. Their format and medium do not make disclosure harmless.
A temporary employee's contract is extended. Unbounded or excessive access is not justified by continued employment alone.
A support engineer needs production access for one repair. Broad standing authority and untracked use increase unnecessary exposure.
A user signs in using federation but the application trusts an assertion for another service. Appearance and public identity details do not resolve effective access.

What is a network port used to identify in common transport protocols?|A logical service endpoint on a host|The legal owner of a device|The physical room containing a server|Ports distinguish service endpoints. They do not establish ownership or physical location.
What is the purpose of routing?|Selecting paths for packets between networks|Approving every business transaction|Removing malware from workstations|Routing directs network traffic. Business authority and malware removal require other mechanisms.
What does a subnet represent?|A logical address grouping within an IP network|A guarantee that every host is trusted|A replacement for all firewall policies|Address grouping supports network organisation. Trust and permitted communications need explicit controls.
What does a VPN usually provide?|A protected tunnel between defined endpoints|Proof that the connecting device is uncompromised|Automatic permission to use every internal service|A VPN protects a connection within its model. Device state and application authority still need validation.
What is the difference between an allow rule and a deny rule?|One permits matching traffic and the other blocks it|Both clean compromised systems|Both prove the user behind a packet|Rules govern matching flows. They do not establish a healthy endpoint or human identity automatically.
Why avoid exposing a device's administration interface to everyone?|It increases who can attempt sensitive management access|Public interfaces cannot use encryption|Only public IP addresses can be logged|Broad reachability expands exposure even when encrypted. Logging and encryption are independent questions.
What is a network zone?|A grouping with defined communication and protection requirements|A guarantee that all devices have equal business value|A room temperature measurement|Zones support explicit boundaries and policy. They need not imply equal asset importance or physical location.
What does a network proxy do in a typical web flow?|Intermediates requests between a client and destination|Necessarily decrypts every connection on earth|Makes every requested site trustworthy|A proxy sits in a request path within its configured scope. Its presence alone neither gives universal visibility nor proves content safety.
What does network latency describe?|Delay experienced as data travels or requests complete|The number of authorised users|The amount of data classified confidential|Latency is a timing measure. User authority and information sensitivity are different properties.
What does packet loss describe?|Packets failing to reach the observed destination|Every packet being copied by an attacker|A user forgetting a password|Loss indicates delivery failure within the observed path. It does not itself establish interception or identity problems.
Why distinguish a listening port from an authorised service?|Reachability does not establish approval or safe configuration|Every listening port is automatically malware|Approved services never use ports|A reachable listener may be legitimate or unintended. Validate ownership, need and configuration rather than infer intent from presence alone.
What should a secure guest network separate?|Guest access from sensitive organisational services unless explicitly justified|Only guests with different phone brands|Every staff identity from all public websites|Separate access according to intended use and exposure. Why protect network equipment configurations?|Changes can affect reachability, security and recovery|Configurations contain only cosmetic settings|Only application servers need backups|Routing and policy settings influence operations. They need controlled access and recoverable configuration records.
What is a network flow record useful for?|Summarising observed communications between endpoints|Reconstructing every byte of every application message|Proving the user intended the traffic|Flow metadata can support analysis but generally is not full content or a human-intent verdict.
Why use an approved naming and addressing inventory?|To connect network observations to managed systems and owners|To guarantee addresses never change|To remove the need for authentication|Inventory supports interpretation and operations. Addresses can still change and access still needs authentication.

Unnecessary reachability and anonymous privilege increase exposure.

Explicit risk treatment is more defensible than hiding the gap or claiming policy as operation.
A supplier subcontractor handles production data outside the reviewed scope. What should assurance include?|Relevant subcontracting, data-flow and responsibility evidence|Assume the primary supplier's report automatically covers every subcontractor|Review only the direct contract and exclude downstream data flows|Dependencies can extend the data and control boundary. Scope and responsibilities need evidence rather than implied inheritance.
Management changes a metric definition midway through a reporting year. What should the report show?|The definition change and how it affects comparison|Present the new measure as comparable without disclosing the definition change|Delete the old definition so only one version can be reviewed|Definitions affect interpretation. Disclose the break or reconcile comparable data rather than imply an unchanged measure.
An internal audit recommendation proposes a named product. How should management evaluate alternatives?|Against the required control outcome, risk and evidence of effectiveness|Treat the named product as the only acceptable implementation|Choose the lowest-price alternative without assessing the control outcome|A recommendation should address the control need. Equivalent justified treatments can be valid, but product labels and price alone do not establish effectiveness.
